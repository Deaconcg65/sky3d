    Web browsers may not read the flight network directly, so it is fetched through a small relay that runs on your receiver. Leave this empty to use the relay on the receiver this page came from.
    Press Enter or Find to look it up. Airport codes are found on this page. Anything else is sent, through this receiver, to the OpenStreetMap place finder: only the words you typed, never your position. If you type a street address, that address is what is sent. Places by OpenStreetMap.

    My places
    Type an address or a city, or paste a latitude and longitude from Google Maps. Each place gets a label on the ground and joins the list above.
    Your places stay on this device. They are never sent to this site or saved on it. Only a rough position, to about seven miles, is used to ask for the aircraft and the weather there. With the box unticked, your places are forgotten when you close this page.
    Type a flight number such as DL1234 or a tail number such as N123AB. Beyond the antenna's reach its position comes from ADSB.lol.
    Up to 10 tail numbers or flight numbers. You get an alert when one is seen anywhere: by the antenna, or by the flight networks, which are asked about each one every two minutes or so. Alerts come only while this page is open.
    Up to 10 tail numbers or flight numbers. You get an alert when the antenna hears one. Alerts come only while this page is open.
    Day, night, dawn and dusk come from this computer's clock and the sunrise and sunset for the place on screen.
